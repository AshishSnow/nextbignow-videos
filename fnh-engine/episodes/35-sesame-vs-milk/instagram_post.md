Caption:
Does sesame have more calcium than milk?
On paper, yes: whole sesame seeds have about 975 mg of calcium per 100 g, milk about 113 mg. That's over 8 times more.
But most of it sits in the seed's thin skin, and a lot of it is bound to oxalate, so your body absorbs only a small part.
And nobody eats 100 g: one spoon of til has about a third of the calcium in a glass of milk.
Til is a lovely bonus, just not a swap for your milk.
Sources: USDA nutrient data (NIH ODS); J Food Composition and Analysis (2004); Bunseki Kagaku (1994); Healthline (RD-reviewed, 2026).
