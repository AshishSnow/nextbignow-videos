## 2. How the platforms rank us (2026)

- **YouTube Shorts**
  - Viewed vs swiped away is decided in the first 1-2 s.
  - Average percentage viewed above 100% (replays) is a strong push.
  - Every replay counts as a view (since Mar 31, 2025).
  - Diagnose:
    - Low "viewed" means the opening is the problem.
    - Good "viewed" but low % watched means the middle is the problem.
- **Instagram Reels**
  - Ranks on watch time, sends per reach and likes per reach.
  - Sends (DM shares) are the top signal for reaching non-followers.
  - The first few seconds decide whether a Reel gets pushed.
  - Original work beats reposts.
- **Script test:** "Would someone DM this to a friend?" If not, change the angle before writing.

## 3. The script (target 30-45 s)

- **Length:** 95-125 spoken words. v1 scripts ran 145-165 words, about 60 s.
- **Pauses:** none longer than about 0.4 s. Dead air over 1 s feels like a mistake.

### 0-2 s: hook

- The first frame is the most striking real visual of the story, already moving, plus at most 3-5 words on screen.
- Spoken line 1 has at most 12 words.
- Hook formulas, in order of what works for us:
  1. **"What if you…" + body/danger:** "What if you dug in the Saudi desert… and hit a hippo?"
  2. **Paradox you can picture:** "This rock is alive. 245 animals live inside it."
  3. **Direct stake:** "Your next phone battery might come from the bottom of the ocean."
- **Never open with:**
  - a date ("On Wednesday…") or an organization ("Scientists at…", "NOAA says…")
  - "This week", or a greeting
  - an abstract noun ("The world's inbox…")
- **Never start on a still frame.**

### 2-6 s: the promise

- One sentence that says what they get by staying: the twist or answer we'll pay off.
- This opens the BIG loop.

### Body: But/Therefore, never "and then"

- Each beat is one new fact that changes the situation. Chain them with BUT or SO; don't stack facts with AND.
- Open a small loop about every 8 s ("…and that's not even the weird part") and close it within the next 1-2 lines.
- Strongest proof early, biggest reveal late. Promise the reveal early so they wait for it.
- One fact per line; at most one number per line, rounded and spoken in plain words.
- Cut hedges ("basically", "essentially", "it's worth noting") and repeated points.
- Mark a visual beat every 2-3 s in the script (cut, sticker, zoom, SFX).

### Ending: payoff + loop

- Pay off the big loop with the "what happens next" line (our channel promise).
- Make the last line run straight into line 1, so the restart feels seamless. That drives replays (YouTube counts each replay as a view) and watch time (IG).
- Skip the spoken "comment yes or no".
  - Put the question in the caption, and say it only if it IS the loop line.
  - IG caption: a send prompt ("Send this to the friend who…").
  - YT: subscribe on the end card, not in the voiceover.

## 4. Topic filter (before research)

A topic is only a GO if it has all of these:
- A person or "you" at the center.
- A physical image a viewer can picture in 1 s.
- A real, checkable case.
- A "what happens next" that touches the viewer's life, money, body or future.

## 5. Writing process (every script)

1. Write 3 hooks, each starting with a different formula. Pick the one that is clearest muted, in 1 s.
2. Write a beat sheet: Promise → But → So → But → Reveal → Next → Loop.
3. Draft the script. Read it aloud and cut 20%.
4. Loop check: read the last line, then the first line, as one sentence.
5. Swipe check, line by line: "If I were bored, would I swipe here?" If yes, cut that line or add a but.
6. After posting, log the 3-s view rate and average watch for that hook formula. Change one variable per video.

## 6. v3 additions from primary sources (creators + platforms)

### Jenny Hoyos (shorts averaging ~10M views; Creator Science podcast, TED)

- **Structure:** HOOK (a few seconds), then FORESHADOW (2 lines, 3 s or less, setting an expectation they want resolved), then TRANSITION, then BODY (but/therefore), then PAYOFF.
- **Write the last line FIRST.** End right after the payoff: "Whatever you say you're gonna do, you end it right after you do it."
  - Cutting 1 second from one ending took retention from 83% to 88%, and that video took off.
- **Reading level:** 5th grade or below. The top shorts she checked scored near 1st grade. Explain any jargon word in plain words.
- **Visible mechanism:** numbered steps, a counter, or "3 things" so viewers know how far they are from the end.
- **Length:** about 34 s is her sweet spot on YouTube.
- **Breakout targets:** about 85% viewed vs swiped (platform average about 70%), and 90%+ average viewed.
- **Idea filter:** strong hook? clear mechanism? will people rewatch?

### Kallaway (hook framework)

- **Hook = curiosity loop in 3 moves:**
  1. **Context lean:** the topic is instantly clear, plus common ground (a benefit or pain, a familiar reference, or a metaphor).
  2. **Scroll-stop interjection:** a short line with "but / however / yet".
  3. **Contrarian snapback:** the payoff turns opposite to the lean, and the payoff must be real.
- **Visual hook beats spoken:** 3-5 bold words on screen plus moderate motion (not chaos, not still).
  - The on-screen text must state the context, not repeat the voice.
- **First "hit of value" within about 4 s.**
- **Staccato:** shortest sentences at the start, longer later.
- **Familiar comparisons make new topics feel safe.** Say "bigger than an IMAX", "the size of a school bus", or a known brand or person. Verbal only: no logos on screen.

### MrBeast production memo

- Show, don't tell: stop saying what they'll see and show it.
- Stair-step the stakes: each beat bigger than the last.
- Never signal the ending early. Endings must be abrupt; a strong payoff lifts retention.
- Keep it simple enough for 50M people to understand.

### Zack D. Films ("How Stitches Work": 31 s, 253M views)

- One question stated in the first second. The video ends the moment it is answered.

### YouTube (Todd Sherman, Shorts lead) and Paddy Galloway (3.3B-view study)

- "The audience is the algorithm."
  - Every Short gets a seed test.
  - Performance is "whether people are choosing to watch and not skip" in the feed.
- Galloway's study:
  - Shorts under 60% viewed vs swiped rarely did well; the best were at 70-90%.
  - Shorts with average watch above 50 s averaged 4.1M views. Holding people matters, not raw length.
- No magic posting time or quota. Original content is prioritized; re-uploads risk being treated as spam.

### South Park rule

- Beats connect with BUT or THEREFORE, never "and then". Each scene causes or complicates the next.

## 7. The v3 template (fill in, in this order)

1. **LAST LINE** (payoff + loop into line 1): written first.
2. **HOOK**
   - Line 1: context lean in 12 words or fewer, "you" or a picture-able paradox.
   - Line 2: BUT, an interjection of 6 words or fewer.
   - Line 3: snapback, the real surprise.
3. **FORESHADOW** (3 s or less): what they'll get by staying, plus a visible mechanism ("3 clues", a countdown, a number climbing).
4. **BODY:** 3-4 beats chained BUT / SO.
   - Stakes rise each beat.
   - One fact and at most one number per line, said with a familiar comparison.
5. **PAYOFF:** the "what happens next" reveal.
6. **CUT:** end on the payoff line, with no sign-off.

### Checks

- Run `tools/script_check.py`: 95-125 words, grade 5 or below, line 1 at most 12 words, no weak opener, 2+ "but".
- Read it aloud and cut 20%.

## 8. v4 (Nov 24 2026): Kallaway "content as science" + our latest numbers

Source: Kallaway's *Short Form Growth System*, intro email. Ashish shared it so we would learn from it.

### 8.1 The 11 Lego bricks, filled in for NextBigNow

Each short is a stack of 11 bricks. To make a new short, keep most of them and swap one or two on purpose. They fall into 5 groups, built in this order:

1. **Format** (one brick): what kind of short it is: news "what happens next", explainer, "what if you" scenario, or survival story.
2. **Idea** (three bricks):
   - **Topic**: the subject.
   - **Seed**: the one-line premise. If you can't write it in one sentence, the idea isn't ready.
   - **Substance**: the facts, numbers and examples that create emotion.
3. **Hook** (three bricks, most important first):
   - **Text hook**: the title on frame 1. It matters most.
   - **Visual hook**: what moves in frame 1.
   - **Spoken hook**: line 1 of the voiceover.
4. **Script** (two bricks):
   - **Story structure**: hook, BUT, snapback, beats, payoff, loop.
   - **CTA**: the caption question plus the send prompt.
5. **Edit** (two bricks):
   - **Visual layout**: tortoise comic look plus 2-3 essay slides.
   - **Elements**: tape labels, bursts, shakes, SFX, subtitles.

### 8.3 What works and what doesn't

**Winning bricks:**
- **Topic:** your body, an animal, or an ancient discovery.
- **Seed:** an absurd image you can picture.
- **Spoken hook:** "you" plus a physical object, either in your hand or one you'd find.

**Losing bricks:**
- Abstract tech, finance or AI news.
- An organization or a date as the opener.
- A brand name as the subject.

**When a money/AI topic still makes sense:** only do it if the seed turns into an object in your hand. For example, "the phone in your pocket just got pricier because of AI", not "AI memory prices doubled".

### 8.4 Hook rules (text hook first)

1. **Text hook (frame-1 title):** 3-6 words.
   - It must add context or a curiosity gap that the voice does not say.
   - Never use a plain topic label. Bad: "WHY FARTS STINK". Better: "99% OF IT IS ODORLESS".
   - The tape label above the title gives the subject ("YOUR BODY"); the title gives the gap.
2. **Visual hook:** the hero object is already moving in frame 1, at the center, big. No empty frame, and no text-only opener.
3. **Spoken hook:** 12 words or fewer. Keep the "you + object" or paradox pattern from section 3.

### 8.5 Seed test (before any research)

Write the seed in one line: `[you/your thing] + [surprising fact] + [why it matters to you]`.

If the seed isn't a picture someone can see in 1 second, change the angle or the topic.

### 8.6 One experiment per video (the scientist loop)

Every episode logs its 11 bricks and **one** improvement hypothesis in `tools/experiments.md`. Example: "text hook states a number instead of a label → expect higher 3s rate."

- Change one brick at a time, so the result tells us something.
- After 48 h or more, fill in 3s rate, average watch, shares and saves from Metricool (IGRE28, IGRE24, IGRE21, IGRE12).
- Keep the brick if it beat the 7-day median; drop it if not.

### 8.7 The 4 "cannot lose" conditions, applied to us

1. **Post consistently.** 5 reels a day at fixed slots (09:40, 12:10, 15:10, 18:10, 20:40 IST).
2. **Get measurably better each rep.** The experiment log in 8.6.
3. **Funnel views into something.** The caption send prompt plus the YT subscribe end card. Revisit this when there's a lead magnet.
4. **Never stop.** Kallaway says skills lock in around rep 50. We're at about ep100, so keep the loop running.
