In late twenty thirty-two, every retiree in America could get a twenty-two percent pay cut. Automatically. Not because of a market crash. Because a fund runs out of money. [dramatic] So what happens if Social Security runs dry?
---
Here's how it works. The money taken out of your paycheck today goes straight out to today's retirees. Whatever's left over sits in a trust fund. For years, that fund kept growing. Now it's shrinking. Fast.
---
In June, the government's own trustees said the retirement fund runs dry in late twenty thirty-two. That's one year sooner than they said last year. After that, the law only lets them pay out what comes in. That's about seventy-eight cents on every dollar.
For a typical couple retiring around then, one budget group puts the cut at about eighteen thousand dollars a year.
---
So why is this happening? Simple math. In nineteen sixty, there were about five workers paying in for every person getting a check. Today, it's under three. And it keeps falling. Fewer babies, people living longer, and a giant wave of retirees.
---
And here's the wild part. This already happened once. In nineteen eighty-three, the fund was just months from running out. Checks could've been late that summer. Congress panicked and made a deal. They slowly raised the full retirement age from sixty-five to sixty-seven, started taxing some benefits, and raised payroll taxes sooner. It was signed in April. Just in time.
---
So what are the options now? To fix it for the next seventy-five years, you'd need to raise the payroll tax by about a third. Or cut benefits by about a quarter. Or some mix of both. And every year Congress waits, the fix gets bigger.
---
So here's my prediction. Social Security doesn't disappear. The checks don't stop. But nothing big happens until the very last minute, just like nineteen eighty-three. Expect a deal right before the deadline, and expect it to be a mix. A bit more money coming in, and smaller promises for younger workers.
