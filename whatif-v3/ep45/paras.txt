January twenty twenty-two. A volcano blew up under the Pacific, and a whole country fell off the internet. [dramatic] Not slow. Gone. So what happens if the cables under the ocean get cut?
---
Here's what most people don't know. Your videos, your messages, your bank app. About ninety-nine percent of the internet between countries doesn't go through space. It runs through cables on the ocean floor. Out in the deep sea, a lot of them are about as thick as a garden hose.
---
That country was Tonga. The eruption ripped up about ninety kilometers of its main cable. Calls abroad, card payments, news from family overseas, all cut. People waited days just to find out if their relatives were okay. The fix took more than five weeks.
---
And it's not just volcanoes. In September twenty twenty-five, a ship in the Red Sea probably dragged its anchor right across four cables at once. At least ten countries felt it, including India, Pakistan and the UAE. By one estimate, about a quarter of the traffic between Asia, Europe and the Middle East got hit.
---
This happens way more than you'd think. About two hundred cable faults happen every year. That's around four a week. And most of them aren't spies. They're fishing boats and anchors.
Now here's the scary part. The whole world has only about sixty ships that can repair these cables. A lot of them are getting old. And repair times have more than doubled in ten years.
---
So why are people worried right now? Because the breaks keep happening in the same hot spots. The Baltic Sea has had eleven disruptions since twenty twenty-three. And this August, two cables off Perth, Australia, were damaged. Police are looking into a tanker.
---
So can't satellites just take over? [skeptical] Not really. Starlink helped Tonga get a few links back. But one big cable carries way more data than satellites can, so they can't replace a whole region.
---
So here's my prediction. The internet doesn't collapse. There are over six hundred cable systems, and traffic finds another path. But one day, one region gets hit hard, maybe for weeks. And the places with only one or two cables are the ones that go dark.
Watch for three things. More repair ships getting built. Navies guarding cables. And big tech companies, like Google, laying their own.
