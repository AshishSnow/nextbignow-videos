What if clicking "I'm not a robot"... handed a hacker your bank password?
This is real, and it's everywhere. It's called ClickFix. You open a normal-looking website, and a box pops up. "Verify you are human." But instead of pictures of traffic lights, it gives you three easy steps. Press Windows and R. Press Control and V. Press Enter.
That's it. You just hacked yourself.
Here's the trick. When you clicked that box, the site secretly copied a command to your clipboard. Windows plus R opens a little Run window. Control V pastes the command. Enter runs it. In seconds, malware grabs your saved passwords, your banking logins, even your crypto.
And it works way too well. Microsoft says this trick was behind forty-seven percent of the attacks its experts handled in twenty twenty-five. More than regular phishing emails. One security team found over seventeen thousand infected websites showing the fake check.
So here's the rule. A real CAPTCHA will never ask you to press keys or paste anything. Ever. If it does, close the tab.
Already did it? Disconnect from the internet, run a security scan, and change your passwords from a different device.
Would you have fallen for it? Yes or no. And send this to the one person in your family who clicks everything.
