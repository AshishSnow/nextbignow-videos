Just over a year ago, Bitcoin hit a hundred and twenty-six thousand dollars. This week, it slipped to around sixty-six thousand. That's almost half its value, gone. So what happens if Bitcoin keeps falling?
---
Here's what happened. Since late September, about two trillion dollars has been wiped off the whole crypto market. Why? Money got more expensive. The ten-year Treasury yield jumped above five percent, traders started betting on another rate hike, and the dollar got stronger. When safe bonds pay you five percent, a coin that pays you nothing suddenly looks a lot less exciting.
---
And here's what makes it fall even faster. A lot of traders borrow money to bet on Bitcoin. When the price drops, those bets get force-closed, and that selling pushes the price down even more. On Friday alone, more than a billion dollars of crypto bets got wiped out.
---
But we've seen this movie before. In November twenty twenty-one, Bitcoin hit sixty-nine thousand dollars. A year later, it was near fifteen and a half thousand. Down more than seventy-five percent. Then FTX, one of the biggest crypto exchanges in the world, collapsed in a matter of days. Billions of dollars of customer money were missing. Its founder was later convicted of fraud.
---
And yet Bitcoin didn't die. Three years later, it hit a brand new record. That's the wild thing about Bitcoin. It has crashed more than seventy-five percent at least three times, and so far, it has come back every single time.
---
So here's my prediction. Bitcoin is not going to zero this week. Too many big funds and companies now hold it. But this time, there's a new risk. Companies that borrowed money to buy Bitcoin could get squeezed if the price keeps sliding. Watch them. If one of them is forced to sell, that's when things get ugly.
