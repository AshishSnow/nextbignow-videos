For thirty-three years, one man ran Nvidia. On Thursday, he was gone. So what happens now, to the company behind almost every big AI on the planet?
---
Quick catch-up. Jensen Huang co-founded Nvidia in nineteen ninety-three, and turned a graphics card company into the engine of the AI boom. Nvidia sells an estimated ninety percent of the advanced chips that AI runs on.
Then came a week of headlines about his personal life. He admitted he'd been unfaithful, and reports linked him to a relationship with an executive who reported to him. Nvidia didn't give a reason. It just said he resigned, as CEO and from the board, effective immediately.
---
The money reacted first. At one point on Thursday, Nvidia's stock fell fourteen percent. [surprised] Then buyers jumped back in and cut that loss to about four.
For now, the finance chief, Colette Kress, is running the company. She's been CFO since twenty thirteen, while revenue went from about four billion dollars a year to over two hundred billion.
---
So, does AI slow down? Here's my take. Not soon. Chips like Nvidia's are planned years in advance. The next ones are already designed, and the biggest tech companies have already placed huge orders.
The real risk is slower. Founders make the big, weird bets that nobody else would. And without Jensen, there's no one at the top who's been right about the future for thirty years in a row.
---
History gives us clues. When Steve Jobs stepped down, people said Apple was finished. Under Tim Cook, it became the first American company worth a trillion dollars.
When Uber's founder was pushed out in twenty seventeen, a new boss came in, and a few years later Uber turned its first yearly profit.
But it can go the other way too. After Bill Gates handed over Microsoft, it went more than a decade where its stock barely moved.
---
So here's my prediction. Nvidia doesn't collapse. But the next CEO decides if it stays the king of AI, or becomes the next company that used to be.
The first big test comes soon, when Nvidia reports its earnings. Watch that.
