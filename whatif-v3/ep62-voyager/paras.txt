This little machine left Earth in nineteen seventy-seven. And on Wednesday, it will be so far away that light itself needs a full day to reach it.
This is Voyager One. On November eighteenth, it hits one light-day from Earth. Sixteen billion miles. No human-made object has ever been this far from home.
And it is still talking to us. Its radio runs on about twenty watts, roughly the bulb in your fridge. By the time that whisper gets here, NASA needs dishes the size of a football field just to hear it.
Now here's the crazy part. In late twenty twenty-three, Voyager suddenly started sending gibberish. One tiny memory chip had died. So engineers took a computer from the seventies, with no spare parts, and nearly two days of waiting for every reply... and rewrote its code from fifteen billion miles away. In April twenty twenty-four, it spoke again.
But its power is running out. Most of its instruments are already switched off. One day soon, it will go quiet forever... and just keep flying, carrying a golden record of our music, our voices, and a hello in fifty-five languages.
In forty thousand years, it will drift past another star.
So what if someone out there finds it one day? Would you want them to? Yes or no, tell me below.
