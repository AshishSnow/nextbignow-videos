Every single day, America now spends more than three billion dollars just on interest. Not on schools. Not on roads. Just interest. So what happens when a country's debt gets too big? Here's the number. The U.S. owes about forty trillion dollars. This year, the interest bill alone is heading for about one point two trillion. Back in twenty nineteen, it was under four hundred billion. And for a few months, America spent more on interest than on its entire military. So how did it get this bad? Two things. The debt kept growing, and interest rates went up. When rates were near zero, borrowing was almost free. Now, every old loan that gets refinanced costs way more. And the government's own budget office says it gets worse. By twenty thirty-six, interest could hit about two and a half trillion dollars a year. That's roughly double what America will spend on defense. So can a rich country actually get punished for this? Ask Britain. In September twenty twenty-two, the U.K. government announced big tax cuts with no plan to pay for them. Markets panicked. Interest rates on government bonds shot up. The pound crashed to its lowest level ever against the dollar. Some pension funds got so close to collapse that the Bank of England had to step in with an emergency rescue. And the prime minister was gone in just forty-nine days. So here's my prediction. America is not going broke tomorrow. It borrows in its own currency, and the world still wants dollars. But the interest bill becomes the fight of the decade. Expect a showdown between higher taxes, spending cuts, or letting inflation quietly eat the debt.
