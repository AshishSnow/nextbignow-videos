What would happen if you lived alone in a cave for five hundred days? No sunlight. No clock. No phone. Nobody. [dramatic] One woman actually did it. And when her team finally came to get her... she thought they were early.
In November twenty twenty-one, a Spanish mountaineer named Beatriz Flamini climbed down into a cave near Granada, seventy meters underground. The rule was simple. Nobody talks to her. Not even if someone in her family dies. [sarcastic] She did have a panic button. She never touched it.
---
So what happens to your body down there? First, your clock breaks. Your brain has a built-in clock, and it runs on about twenty-four hours and eleven minutes. Every morning, sunlight resets it. In a cave, there is no sunlight. So the clock just... drifts.
A French scientist, Michel Siffre, tested this in nineteen seventy-two. He lived in a cave in Texas for two hundred and five days. Sometimes his body stretched into a forty-eight hour day, and he didn't even notice. [chuckle] Imagine staying up for two days and thinking it's a normal Tuesday.
And Beatriz? She tried to count the days. On day sixty-five, she gave up.
---
Then your memory goes weird. Siffre couldn't remember what he did the day before. And when he counted to one hundred twenty, one number per second, it took him five minutes. [surprised] Five real minutes felt like two.
Beatriz filled the time instead. She read sixty books, knitted hats, exercised, and talked to herself. [chuckle] Not out loud. She says she got on very well with herself.
Her team lowered food down a rope, and pulled her waste back up. [sarcastic] Best room service ever.
Then came the worst day. Flies. The cave got invaded, and she says she was completely covered in them.
---
Finally, in April twenty twenty-three, her team climbed down. She was asleep. When they woke her, she said: [surprised] Already? No way.
In her head, about one hundred and sixty days had passed. The real number? Five hundred. Her brain had lost almost a year.
[happy] And her first words about leaving? She didn't want to. She hadn't finished her book.
So why do this? Scientists are using her recordings to study how isolation changes sleep, memory and time. That matters for astronauts on long missions.
[chuckle] So next time you lose track of time on your phone, relax. At least you can see the sun.
