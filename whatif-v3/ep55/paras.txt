Last Thursday, Microsoft's email went down around the world. For almost a full day. In Japan, some traders went back to fax machines. So what happens if the cloud goes down for a whole week?
---
Here's what happened. On November twelfth, Outlook, Teams and Copilot started failing. Nearly a hundred and thirty thousand people reported problems on Downdetector. It hit the U.S. House of Representatives, parts of Britain's health service, and government systems in Japan and Australia. Microsoft says it wasn't a cyberattack. It was a network change that went wrong.
---
And that's the scary part. No hacker. No bomb. Just one bad change. Because today, millions of companies run on the same few clouds. Microsoft, Amazon, and Google. When one of them sneezes, the whole world catches a cold.
---
We've seen how bad it can get. In July twenty twenty-four, a security company called CrowdStrike pushed one faulty update. About eight and a half million Windows computers crashed with the blue screen of death. Some airports went back to handwritten boarding passes. Delta alone canceled thousands of flights. And one estimate put the losses for big U.S. companies at more than five billion dollars. The bug was fixed in hours. The cleanup took days.
---
So here's my prediction. A full week offline is unlikely. These companies have backups for their backups. But big outages keep coming. So smart companies will stop trusting just one cloud. Expect a boom in backup plans, from a second cloud provider to old-school offline tools. Maybe even the fax machine.
