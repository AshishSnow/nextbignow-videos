What if you went over Niagara Falls... inside a barrel? Sounds like a terrible idea, right? Well, the first person who actually survived it was a sixty-three-year-old schoolteacher. And she did it on her birthday.
Her name was Annie Edson Taylor. It was nineteen oh one, and she was almost broke. Her plan? Do something nobody had ever survived, get famous, and never worry about money again.
[chuckle] She even told reporters she was forty-three. Twenty years younger. Bold move.
---
So she built a custom barrel. Oak wood, iron rings, about as tall as a person. Inside, she put a mattress, a leather harness, and a heavy anvil at the bottom, so it would land the right way up.
Two days before her ride, she tested it... on her cat. [surprised] The cat went over the falls first. And it came back alive, with a cut on its head.
---
On October twenty-fourth, her friends sealed her in and pumped extra air inside with a bicycle pump. Then they pushed her into the river.
She floated for about twenty minutes, spinning toward the edge. [dramatic] Then she went over a fifty-one meter drop. That's like falling off a seventeen-story building.
At the bottom, you're hitting the water at around a hundred and ten kilometers per hour. And it's not calm water down there. More than an Olympic pool's worth of water crashes down every single second.
---
When they finally opened the barrel, Annie climbed out. A cut on her head, bruised, shaking... but alive. [happy] The first person ever to survive going over Niagara Falls.
And her first words were basically: never do this. She said she'd rather walk up to a cannon than go over those falls again.
---
So did she get rich? [sarcastic] Nope. Her manager ran off with the famous barrel. She spent the rest of her life posing for photos with tourists and selling little books about her ride. She died broke, at eighty-two.
Today, going over the falls is illegal, and people have died trying. So please don't. Just ask yourself one thing. Would you have gone second, after the cat? Tell me in the comments.
