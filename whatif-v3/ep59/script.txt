This week, a rocket came back from space, and a giant tower caught it. With two metal arms. Right out of the sky. So what happens when rockets become as reusable as airplanes? Here's what happened. SpaceX launched Starship on Friday evening from Texas. The top half, the ship, flew around the Earth for about twenty-two hours and dropped off twenty-six satellites. Then it came back through the atmosphere, slowed down, and the launch tower grabbed it in midair. It's the first time anyone has ever caught a spacecraft coming back from orbit. SpaceX called it the missing link. Why does that matter? Because for most of history, rockets were thrown away. Imagine flying from New York to London, and then throwing the plane in the ocean. That's how spaceflight worked. Every launch meant building a brand-new rocket. We've seen what reuse can do. The Space Shuttle was supposed to fix this. It was partly reusable, but every flight still cost around one and a half billion dollars, because it needed months of repairs. Then, in December twenty fifteen, SpaceX landed a Falcon 9 booster back on the ground for the first time. People said it was impossible.
