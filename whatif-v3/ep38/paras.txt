What if your ship flipped over... and sank to the bottom of the sea with you still inside? No way out. Pitch black. That's exactly what happened to a ship's cook named Harrison Okene.
It was May twenty thirteen, off the coast of Nigeria. At four thirty in the morning, a huge wave hit his tugboat and flipped it upside down. Harrison was in the bathroom, wearing nothing but his shorts.
[dramatic] The boat sank thirty meters down... and landed on the seafloor, upside down.
---
Water was pouring in everywhere. Harrison felt his way through the dark, pushed into a small office... and found something amazing. A pocket of air, trapped against the ceiling. Just over a meter tall.
So he did the only thing he could. He stuck his head in that bubble... and waited.
---
Now here's the scary part. Every breath you take puts carbon dioxide into the air. In a small bubble, that gas builds up fast, and too much of it can kill you.
[surprised] But Harrison got lucky. The water pressure squeezed the air in that pocket, so it held more oxygen than it looked. And the splashing water soaked up some of that carbon dioxide.
Then there was the cold. Sitting in cold water can drain your body heat in just a few hours. So he pulled a mattress over and built himself a little platform, to keep most of his body out of the water.
All he had was a bottle of Coca-Cola, a life jacket, and two tiny flashlights. [whispering] And total darkness, for hours and hours.
---
Almost three days later, divers came down. But they weren't looking for survivors. They were there to recover bodies.
One diver swam through the wreck, reached out... [dramatic] and a hand grabbed his. [surprised] Harrison was alive!
After about sixty hours underwater, he couldn't just swim up. His body was full of pressurized gas, so he had to go into a decompression chamber first, and let his body come back to normal pressure slowly.
---
[happy] He thought he'd only been down there for one day. He'd actually been trapped for almost three.
