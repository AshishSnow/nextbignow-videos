Last Thursday, the man who built Nvidia walked out the door. Effective immediately. Thirty-three years as CEO, gone in one announcement. So what happens to a giant company when its legend suddenly leaves?
---
Here's what we know. Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's CEO and left its board. Nvidia didn't give a reason. It came in the middle of a personal scandal, and the board has launched an independent investigation. Nvidia's stock fell as much as fourteen percent in a single day.
---
Why does this matter so much? Nvidia makes the chips behind almost the entire AI boom. Huang co-founded it in nineteen ninety-three, and for a lot of investors, he was Nvidia. Now the company is run by its chief financial officer, Colette Kress, as interim CEO. While she ran the money, Nvidia's yearly revenue grew from about four billion dollars to more than two hundred billion.
---
We've seen a legend leave before. In August twenty eleven, Steve Jobs stepped down as Apple's CEO. People said Apple was finished without him. Tim Cook took over. Seven years later, Apple became the first U.S. company worth a trillion dollars. The lesson? Great founders build a machine, not just a myth.
---
So here's my prediction. Nvidia doesn't collapse. The world still needs its chips, and the orders are already in. But picking the next CEO is a huge deal. The first real test comes on November seventeenth, when Nvidia reports earnings. If the numbers are strong, this becomes a footnote. If they're weak, the market will blame the empty chair. And no, this isn't financial advice.
