What if a war ended... and nobody told you? Imagine hiding in a jungle, still following orders, for twenty-nine years. That really happened to a Japanese soldier named Hiroo Onoda.
In December nineteen forty-four, he was sent to Lubang, a small island in the Philippines. His orders were clear. Never surrender. Keep going, no matter what. Even if it takes years.
---
Just a few months later, in nineteen forty-five, World War Two ended. Planes dropped leaflets over the jungle saying the war was over. [surprised] Onoda read them... and decided they were fake. An enemy trick.
His family sent letters and photos. People shouted through loudspeakers. He didn't believe any of it.
---
So he stayed. Living in the jungle, moving camp all the time, cleaning his rifle every day. Years went by. Then decades.
It wasn't harmless. Onoda and his men kept attacking people on the island, because they thought they were still at war. Local farmers were hurt and killed. One by one, his own companions surrendered or died. In the end, he was alone.
---
Then, in nineteen seventy-four, a young Japanese explorer named Norio Suzuki went looking for him. [chuckle] His plan, in his own words, was to find Lieutenant Onoda, a panda, and the Abominable Snowman. In that order.
And he actually found Onoda! In just four days. But Onoda still refused to give up. He'd only stop if his commanding officer ordered him to.
[surprised] So Suzuki went back to Japan... found his old commander, now working in a bookshop... and brought him all the way to the jungle.
---
On March ninth, nineteen seventy-four, the commander read him his new orders. Stop fighting. After twenty-nine years, Onoda handed over his rifle, still in working condition, and his sword.
[dramatic] He'd missed the moon landing. Color TV. The Beatles. A whole world had changed without him.
---
Later, he moved to Brazil to raise cattle, then started a nature school in Japan, teaching kids how to survive outdoors. He lived to ninety-one.
