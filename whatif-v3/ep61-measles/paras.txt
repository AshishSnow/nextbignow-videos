What if a virus America wiped out twenty-six years ago... just moved back in?
This week, it did. The Pan American Health Organization says the United States has lost its measles elimination status. The rule is simple. If one chain of measles keeps spreading for twelve straight months, it is not eliminated anymore.
Look at the jump. Two hundred eighty-five cases in 2024. Two thousand two hundred eighty-nine in 2025. This year, more than five thousand four hundred. Pennsylvania alone has over fifteen hundred. And ten people have died this year, including babies too young for the shot.
Why is it so hard to stop? Measles is one of the most contagious viruses we know. It can hang in the air of a room for up to two hours after a sick person leaves. To block it, about ninety-five percent of people need to be immune. Right now, ninety-two point four percent of kindergartners are fully vaccinated, and only ten states hit the target.
Canada lost the same status one year ago.
But there is a comeback story. In 2019, New York gave about sixty thousand MMR shots in a few months, and stopped its outbreak.
