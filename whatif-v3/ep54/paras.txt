Two weeks ago, someone in Washington state died of bird flu. They caught it from infected dairy cows. That makes three Americans killed by it so far. So what happens if bird flu learns to spread between people?
---
First, the good news. Right now, it does not spread from person to person. About seventy Americans have caught it since twenty twenty-four, and almost all of them worked with sick cows or chickens. Officials say the risk to the public is low.
But the virus is everywhere. It has been found in dairy cows in nineteen states. And since twenty twenty-two, America has lost well over a hundred and fifty million chickens and other birds to it. Remember when eggs hit six dollars a dozen? That's why.
---
So why are scientists nervous? Every time this virus infects a mammal, it gets another chance to change. And it only takes a few small changes for a flu virus to start jumping from person to person.
---
We've seen a brand-new flu go global before. In April two thousand nine, a new swine flu showed up in Mexico and the U.S. Within two months, the World Health Organization called it a pandemic. In its first year, the CDC estimates about sixty million Americans got sick. And the vaccine? It didn't arrive until October. Six months after the virus.
---
But this time, there's one big difference. We are watching early. Scientists already have bird flu vaccines ready to scale, and some countries have started stockpiling doses.
---
So here's my prediction. Bird flu is not the next pandemic tomorrow. But the longer it spreads in cows, the more chances it gets. If it ever does jump between people, the winners will be the countries that test fast and vaccinate fast. And no, this isn't medical advice. But maybe skip the raw milk.
