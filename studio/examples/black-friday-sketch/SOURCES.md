# Sources: why it's called Black Friday

- History.com, Sarah Pruitt, "The Real History of Black Friday": https://www.history.com/articles/black-friday-thanksgiving-origins-history
  - "In the 1950s, police in Philadelphia used the term to describe the chaos that ensued on the day after Thanksgiving" (crowds of suburban shoppers and tourists before the Army-Navy game that Saturday; police worked extra-long shifts).
  - Merchants "tried unsuccessfully to change it to 'Big Friday'" (by 1961).
  - The "go into the black" profit story is "officially sanctioned—but inaccurate"; "Sometime in the late 1980s, however, retailers found a way to reinvent Black Friday".
- Britannica, "Why Is It Called Black Friday?": https://www.britannica.com/story/why-is-it-called-black-friday
- NRF press release, 2 Dec 2025: "A record 202.9 million consumers shopped during the five-day holiday weekend from Thanksgiving Day through Cyber Monday" ("two hundred and three million").
  https://nrf.com/media-center/press-releases/thanksgiving-holiday-weekend-draws-a-record-203-million-shoppers
