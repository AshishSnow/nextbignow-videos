# OMB long-form key (YouTube 5–6 min case studies) — learned 27 Nov 2026
Sources: Paddy Galloway (New Rules of YouTube, 2025 transcript), OutlierKit (faceless business-documentary teardown; hooks & retention), George Blackman (setup → tension → payoff), CreatorSkills long-form script system, humanizer, ghost-editor / Motion Video Director.

## 1. Topic first (before any script)
A topic qualifies only if ALL are true:
1. **Familiar**: the viewer already knows the company or person (KFC, Sears, Ford). Unknown companies make the video build curiosity from zero.
2. **One central decision**: we can say it in one sentence ("he sold KFC for $2M at 73 and kept only his face"). If we can't, the story drifts.
3. **Outcome-reveal title exists**: "How X lost Y", "He sold X for $2M, then…". Familiar + one unexpected element.
4. **Proven demand**: other channels covered the subject and did well, so people want it. We win with a NEW angle, not a new subject.
5. **Primary sources**: court filings, annual reports, period newspaper quotes. At least two sources per fact.
6. **Visuals we can actually get**: free photos of the person and the place (pre-1929 = public domain, US government, Wikimedia), or the owner supplies them. Check this BEFORE writing.
7. **Not in topics.md.**
Use the 80% rule: 4 of 5 videos on the proven format; 1 experiment.

## 2. Packaging before script (Galloway)
- Write 30–40 title options in ~10 min; start with the most exaggerated, then pull back to what's true.
- Proven devices: outcome reveal, superlatives (first, biggest), versus, a real number, a name plus a descriptor ("KFC's founder").
- The thumbnail line is 2–4 words and different from the title (title = story, thumbnail = feeling: "HE HATED KFC").
- The intro must confirm the click within ~7 seconds and add a NEW hook by ~30 seconds.

## 3. Script structure for 5–6 min (~150 wpm → 750–850 words)
| Beat | Time | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open | 0:00–0:30 | Prove the title in 7 s with the most shocking real moment or number. Then open the main question. No greeting, no logo, no "in this video". |
| Setup | 0:30–1:30 | Only the context needed: who, what they built, why it worked. Compress the backstory; no job lists unless they matter. |
| The decision | 1:30–2:45 | The one choice the whole video hinges on, with stakes. Retention holds or collapses here. |
| Unravel | 2:45–4:15 | Consequences in sequence, each caused by the last ("so", "but", never "and then"). |
| Aftermath | 4:15–5:15 | Where money, people and brand ended up, with concrete numbers. Put the strongest moment around two-thirds of the way (≈3:40). |
| Lesson | 5:15–5:45 | One transferable principle in plain words, then end. One closing line, no recap. |

Inside every segment: **setup → tension → payoff** (say why the next 30–60 s matters, reveal it step by step, land a clear "oh"). A new surprise every 20–30 s. Re-hook at every segment change ("That was the easy part. The next decision cost him $283 million."). At most 2 open loops at a time; close each within 1–2 min (short video). Never say "stay to the end" or signal the ending.

## 4. Writing style
- Write the way you'd tell a friend. Short sentences, concrete nouns, real numbers.
- Cut AI tells: staged openers ("Let's go back to the beginning"), a one-line punchline after every section, forced triads, "not X but Y" for drama, hype words.
- One mirror/payoff line in the whole video, at the end.
- Sequence, don't summarize: every paragraph must change the situation.

## 5. Visual grammar (16:9)
- A visual change every 5–10 s (new photo, push-in, caption, map, chart). A still held for 30 s loses people.
- Archive photos as prints with one accent colour; the person cut out with a white die-cut edge; numbers drawn as charts with the source in small type.
- On-screen text ≤ 6–8 words. Chapter cards at each beat (they double as YouTube chapters).
- Hits and whooshes on the first frame of a new scene only. No music (owner rule) unless asked.

## 6. QA before scheduling
Duration = voice; transcript matches the script; every number has two sources in PACKAGING.md; chapters listed in the description; a 1280×720 thumbnail with the person's cut-out and the 2–4 word line.
