# OMB Channel Key (26 Nov 2026)
Built from the following:
- Our own numbers: Mind Tricks gets 1,490–1,894 IG views against 145–207 for founder and case reels, and 27–30% skip against 41–58%.
- The owner's two reference reels.
- Paddy Galloway (outliers), Kallaway (hooks), the MrBeast production memo, Mario Joos (retention editing), Mosseri (Instagram ranking), and Berger & Milkman (what gets shared).

## 1. The one rule
**People don't share information. They share a feeling about themselves.**
Instagram ranks Reels mainly on watch time and **sends**, and sends to non-followers count the most (Mosseri).
A reel gets sent when it makes the sender look smart ("did you know…"), or when it is useful to the friend they send it to ("this is literally you").
Every topic and every last line has to give someone a reason to press send.

## 2. Topic finder: score every idea 0–2 on six things, and only make it at 9+/12
| Test | 2 points if… |
|---|---|
| **Familiar** | The viewer touched or used the thing this week (cart, receipt, Wi-Fi, menu, gas pump), not just "a famous company". |
| **Flip** | One sentence flips a belief they hold ("carts were invented to make you buy more, and nobody wanted them"). |
| **You** | It is happening to *them* (their money, brain or habits). This is why Mind Tricks beats founder stories 8x. |
| **Wow fact** | It contains one detail so odd that people repeat it ("he hired fake shoppers"). |
| **Visual** | We can *show* it with real photos or archive material, or with a prop that explains itself. |
| **Send line** | It ends with an obvious person to send it to ("the friend who's always just grabbing milk"). |

Where to find these ideas:
- **Outliers.** Find a video doing 5–15x its channel's median and copy the *variable* that made it work (topic, angle or format), never the script.
- **Our own winners.** Decoy price, the IKEA effect and the jam study all share one shape: an everyday buying moment, a hidden trick, and a number.
- **"Invented-by" origins** of everyday objects that hide a sales trick: carts, checkout candy, receipts, store music, menu layout, free refills, loyalty cards.
- **Avoid** founder biographies, comeback stories, and anything the viewer has never touched.

## 3. Script logic (not style)
1. **Context lean:** name the familiar thing ("Every grocery store on Earth has these.").
2. **"But"**: one short line that stops the scroll.
3. **Snapback:** the flip ("nobody would touch them").
4. **Wow fact inside 5 seconds**, as the open loop ("So the inventor hired fake shoppers.").
5. **Story in cause → effect beats.** Each line answers the question the last one raised. Link lines with *so* and *but*, never *and then*. Put one new piece of information every 3–4 seconds.
6. **Make it physical:** a heavy basket, a flimsy cart, a door. Concrete beats abstract.
7. **Re-hook at about 60%**: "But here's the part nobody tells you." This turns the story onto the viewer.
8. **The number that lands on the viewer** (+40%).
9. **A mirror line people will repeat:** same shape, flipped meaning ("A basket tells you when to stop. A cart never does.").
10. **Send line, then end abruptly.** Never signal that the end is coming (MrBeast memo).

## 4. The premium documentary look (what made the references feel expensive)
- **Depth, not flat photos:**
  - A sharp focus area with the rest blurred ("blur some places").
  - A blurred copy of the same photo drifting slower behind it, which reads as 2.5D parallax.
  - A spotlight vignette that pulls the eye to one point.
- **Rich grade:**
  - Deep blacks (lifted only slightly), warm highlights, cool shadows, about 20% less saturation.
  - A glow (halation) on bright areas, fine grain, and soft blur at the top and bottom of the frame.
  - Light leaks across cuts and floating dust for "air".
- **One visual idea per line:** the picture changes when the *meaning* changes, not on a timer (Joos: "the speed at which you introduce progressive novel information").
- **Type:**
  - Small off-white captions with tight letter spacing, placed beside the subject.
  - Big words only for the one or two key words in each beat.
  - Stamps and tag boxes reserved for the wow fact.
- **Real archive material:** public-domain Library of Congress and patent drawings via Wikimedia (slow downloads with polite gaps), and line-art props drawn in the same style as the patent ink.
- **Brand everywhere:**
  - The "O." badge top-right.
  - The series colour block with the issue number top-left.
  - The series colour on every highlight.
  - The wordmark on the final frame.

## 5. Checklist before rendering
- Topic scores 9+ on the six tests in section 2.
- The wow fact lands inside 5 s.
- There is a re-hook at about 60%.
- There is a mirror line.
- There is a send line.
- Each fact has two sources.
- Each beat has one visual idea.
- Contact sheet passes: no text in the bottom 420 px, nothing under the right-hand buttons, the brand badge is visible.

## 6. Reference 3 (launch film for "Sol", an email app; 104 s) — emotion over information
- **Arc:**
  - Universal truth: "Everything we make evolves."
  - Fast montage of things everyone knows: light, the map, money, Pokémon, music, the computer.
  - "And this?" narrows to the subject, with a wry, funny line.
  - The pain: "Not one in 50 years made getting started any easier."
  - Then "Until now.", the calm reveal, and reassurance ("None of it sent. That part is still you.").
  - The close calls back to the opening line.
- **Words:** about 89 words/min, half our pace. Fragments. Rhythm of three ("The doc. Created. The time. Found. The reply. Drafted."). Contrast pairs ("Half your inbox took 50 years. The other half starts today.").
- **Cutting tells the emotion:** 57 cuts in the first 57 s (bursts of 2-frame flashes), then only one cut in the last 47 s. Chaos at the start, calm at the end.
- **Colour tells the story:** old archive textures (sepia, black-and-white, CRT scanlines) → saturated neon (now) → pure white with a soft pastel glow (the answer).
- **Type:** one clean geometric sans, small, sentence case, ending with a period, lots of empty space. Small yellow subtitles at the bottom of the picture.
- **Connection:** nostalgia anchors (the Pokémon evolution screen, an office-rage GIF), the viewer's own dock icons and inbox. It is about *their* day.
- **Use for OMB:**
  - Open some reels with a one-line truth plus a montage of five familiar objects.
  - Slow the voice at the emotional turn and hold the shot.
  - Change the colour palette per act.
  - End by calling back to the first line.

## 7. Reference 4 (creator tutorial on hand-drawn text animation; 111 s, 209 wpm, 53 cuts)
- **One signature colour:** every product shot is on the same red paper. A viewer recognises the channel in one frame. For us: one paper colour per series (Mind Tricks = deep purple paper).
- **Tactile, handmade feel:** paper sticker cut-outs (iPad, app icons) with white borders and soft shadows, placed by hands. Real texture beats flat vector.
- **Hand-drawn "boiling" text** at 15 fps: each word redrawn on every frame, starting as a scribble that grows into the word. It looks human and nobody else's titles look like it.
- **Captions:** a small, elegant *italic serif*, one phrase at a time, low-centre. Big type only for the hero word ("Finally", "TEXT ANIMATIONS").
- **Shot rhythm:** a wide shot of the creator in the room, top-down hands, a macro of the screen, and a prop on paper, rotating every 1–2 s. Background lens blur on the wide shots.
- **Value:** exact steps with numbers (15 fps; 2 s = 30 frames), so people save it.
- **CTA:** "Comment PLUGIN and I'll send it to you". Comment-keyword DMs drive comments, which in turn drive reach. We can use this only if the owner sets up DM automation; otherwise keep the send line.

## 8. Topic bank (scored against the 6 tests in section 2)
- 12/12 **Diamond engagement rings were an ad campaign.** De Beers hired N.W. Ayer in 1938, when roughly 10% of engagement rings had a diamond. Copywriter Frances Gerety wrote "A Diamond Is Forever" in 1947, and the share reached about 80% by 1990. ("Two months' salary" came later from De Beers ads.) Sources: Sotheby's, National Geographic, The Drum. Send line: "the friend who's about to propose".
- 11/12 **The shopping cart and the fake shoppers** (made 26 Nov).
- 11/12 **Costco's $1.50 hot dog**, the same price since 1985, kept on purpose as a loss leader (the founder's reported "kill you" quote must be checked verbatim before use).
- 10/12 **Bacon and eggs as "America's breakfast"** came from Edward Bernays' 1920s PR campaign for a bacon company.

## 9. Hook upgrade (owner feedback, 26 Nov: "hook in 5 sec")
- Copy the opening of reference 4: **one designed frame per spoken word for the first ~5 s.**
  - Paper colours alternate: cream / series colour.
  - Each frame uses a different type treatment: a selection box with handles, a huge italic serif word, a dashed guide line, a hand-drawn arrow onto the real object.
- **Point at the real object in second 1** ("You push this every week." + an arrow onto a real cart photo).
- The wow word lands on screen by ~5–6 s ("FAKE SHOPPERS").
- **The owner liked** the series paper colour, the italic serif captions + condensed boiling hero words, the logo/badge, and the sticker look. Keep these.
- **Real images:** fetch Wikimedia **standard-size thumbnails (1280 px) directly** with a policy-compliant User-Agent (tools_ref/thumbget.py). The API search and original files get 429s; thumbnails mostly work.

## 10. Voice (26 Nov owner: "voice looks so much AI")
- **Measured against the references:** our Chatterbox+PSOLA voice paused ~31 times a minute (a pause after every sentence), and its pitch swung 16 st with fast wobble (sing-song, robotic). The references flow in long phrases with few pauses and a natural pitch range.
- **New engine: Maya1** (maya-research/maya1, Apache-2.0; the voice is *designed from a text description*, with no reference speaker or cloning). It runs on CPU via llama.cpp Q4_K_M GGUF plus the SNAC-24k ONNX decoder (studio/lib/maya_tts.py, maya_vo.py). About 18x slower than real time, so roughly 20 min for a 60 s reel.
- **Method:**
  - Generate in **paragraphs** (2–3 sentences), not line by line, so the delivery flows.
  - Cap silences at 0.26 s.
  - Re-align words with Whisper small.en.
  - No PSOLA pitch exaggeration.
  - Result: 189 wpm, 6.5 st range, flowing.
- **Next:** try the inline tags (<chuckle>, <excited>, <sigh>) at emotional beats, and test seeds/descriptions per channel.
