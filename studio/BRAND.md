## 1. What sponsors actually buy (and what we measure from today)
- **Recent views, not followers.** Short-form deals are priced on the average views of the last 10–15 videos (finance/business
  Shorts CPM roughly $25–100 — Creators Agency, May 2026). A long tail of 150-view reels pulls that average down → **fewer, better
  videos beat more videos** (see §5 cadence).
- **Reactions per reach.** Instagram's own top signals are watch time, likes per reach and **sends per reach** — sends drive reach
  to non-followers (Mosseri via Social Media Today, Jan 2025). Our 16 animated reels: ~0 sends, ~0 saves, ~0 comments → reach fell
  ~1,300 → ~150 per reel. Every episode is now built to be sent (a surprising true story + a debatable choice) and answered
  (comment prompt) — STORY.md.
- **Audience quality.** Brands check country/age (US/UK/EU adults is a premium audience), follower authenticity (never buy
  followers/views/engagement; no follow-for-follow) and comment quality (real conversations, not spam).
- **A recognisable format + character.** Animated channels sell a character and a series a brand can sit inside, with no
  personal-scandal risk. That is why STORY.md fixes the hero (orange hoodie = you), the signature beats and three named series.
- **Platform programs** (eligibility as of Oct 2026):
  - YouTube Creator Partnerships (ex-BrandConnect, renamed 23 Mar 2026) needs **YPP**: expanded tier = 500 subscribers + 3 public
    uploads in 90 days + 3M Shorts views in 90 days; full tier = 1,000 subs + 10M Shorts views in 90 days (YouTube Help 13429240,
    9385307). Sharing audience insights with brands increases how often a channel is shown in brand search (YouTube Blog, Mar 2026).
  - Instagram creator marketplace: 18+, public professional account, monetization policies, eligible country; any follower minimum
    is disputed (0–1,000 depending on source).
  - YouTube Open Call (invite-only) may auto-reject AI-generated *visuals* — ours are code-drawn animation; keep it that way.

## 2. Platform policy guardrails that protect the channel
- **YouTube "inauthentic content"** (renamed Jul 2025): channels of near-identical narrated stories with minor changes can lose
  monetization. Series are fine; **every episode must be a genuinely different story with its own scenes, props, music genre and
  structure choices** — never a template clone with the names swapped. The run log must note what is new in each episode.
- **AI labels:** Instagram — keep `isAiGenerated=true` (synthetic narrator voice). YouTube — animation is "non-realistic", keep
  `isAiGeneratedContent=false` unless a scene realistically recreates a real event or person.
- **Never** imitate a real person's voice or likeness; real people appear as generic cartoon characters with role labels.

## 3. Brand-safety rules (a sponsor must be comfortable next to any episode)
1. **True and sourced** — every number verified (PLAYBOOK §3), sources in the post; quotes only from documents.
2. **Curious, not cruel** — no mocking real private people, no "evil company" framing, no punching down; blunders are told as events
   with what courts/regulators found, stated as such ("the court ruled…", "settled without admitting liability").
3. **Topics we skip:** deaths/tragedies as the core of a story, violence, crime glorification, politics/elections, religion, medical or
   health claims, sexual content, gambling/betting promotion, crypto, investment tips ("buy this stock"), get-rich-quick framing.
4. **Language:** family-friendly, no profanity, no shock thumbnails that misrepresent the story.
5. **Don't burn future sponsor categories:** business software, website builders, e-commerce, learning apps, budgeting tools,
   privacy/VPN and newsletters are our most likely sponsors — tell their category's blunders fairly, never as "all X are scams".
6. **No real logos or trade dress** in unsponsored episodes (brand names as plain text are fine); no implied endorsement.

## 4. How a sponsor will fit (design for it now, sell it later)
- **Formats that suit animated story shorts:** (a) *"Presented by"* a whole series ("Your Move, presented by …") — opener badge + end
  card; (b) *in-story integration* — the hero uses the product at the natural "how to beat it" moment of SPOT THE TRICK or the
  takeaway of YOUR MOVE (8–15 s, never in the first 3 s hook, never changing the facts); (c) a recurring segment ("Trick of the
  week"). Usage rights (brand runs our video as an ad) are priced separately.
- **Best-fit categories:** business software/SaaS, website builders, e-commerce platforms, learning platforms, budgeting/personal
  finance tools (non-investment), privacy/VPN, newsletters. **Finance products** only if the brand handles approval (UK FCA FG24/1
  requires sign-off by an authorised firm; no crypto in France without AMF approval).
- **Disclosure (when paid or gifted anything):** on screen from the first second ("Ad · Paid partnership with X"), said aloud in the
  first 5 s, caption starts with "Ad:", Instagram paid-partnership label on, YouTube "includes paid promotion" ticked (since 3 Sep 2026 YouTube may auto-label undeclared branded content, reported secondhand: ALGORITHM.md) and "(Ad)" in
  the title for UK viewers (FTC Endorsement Guides 2023: "difficult to miss"; ASA Aug 2026; EU UCPD; France: "Collaboration
  commerciale" visible throughout). Platform labels alone are not enough.
- **Rates (ranges, price on views):** nano (<10K) ~$20–500 per Reel, micro (10–100K) ~$750–5,000 (US), mid ~$5–20K
  (Influencer Marketing Hub / Later / Modash, 2026). Shorts integrations ~0.25–0.5× a long-form integration.
