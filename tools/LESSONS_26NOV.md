## 2. Script logic shared by all 4 references
1. **Line 1 is a claim, not a list**. It names the category and the stakes: "This is the man the government calls when the economy's about to collapse." / "Everything we make evolves." / "Today I'm finally showing you how I make mine."
2. **A familiar comparison within 6 s** (Godfather, Tony Stark, light/maps/money/Pokémon, the finished effect). It maps the unknown onto something you already know.
3. **The name comes late (~9 s)**, once the viewer already cares.
4. **One irony thesis sentence**, and the whole video exists to resolve it: "the thing that made him X is the same thing that got him laughed at" / "made mail better at arriving, not one made starting easier".
5. **Every sentence is a picture**: "bartenders were becoming mortgage brokers", "a $10,000 donation to a meme page". No abstract lines.
6. **A turn every 10-15 s**: "But instead of...", "Then 2008 hit", "Until now.", "But if you don't have...".
7. **Pattern and repeat**: "Same call. Same man." / "The doc. Created. The time. Found."
8. **A mirrored last line** that reuses earlier words, flips them and points at the viewer: "built the internet you scroll... built the thing quietly watching you do it." No "follow for more".
9. **Pace is a choice, not a rule**: 95 wpm (Sol, with silence) and 190-209 wpm (the others) both work, because the picture changes every 0.7-1.7 s.

## 3. Visual logic
1. **A new picture on every phrase**: 53-106 cuts per video. Our scenes hold one layout for 4-8 s.
2. **One world per video, and its colour tells the story**: ref4 has the red table throughout; Sol goes from dark, grainy past to a bright white future. The colour arc follows the emotion arc.
3. **Real and tactile beats flat clip-art**: real photos and footage, paper props moved by hands, archive film. Our desks and blocks read as stock icons.
4. **Hero text only on the 3-5 key nouns** (COLLAPSE, SUBPRIME, GOTHAM). Everything else is one small word, or nothing.
5. **Silence screens**: a black or white frame with small words, used for the turns ("Until now.", "15 years later...").
6. **Visual callbacks**: the phone call returns, and the frame (picture frame) returns at the end.
7. **Numbers shown big over a blurred background** (2 SECONDS / 30 FRAMES).
8. **Mixed type**: a condensed grotesque for facts, serif italic for the emotional word.

## 4. Rules for the next video
- Format: free to try new formats and topics; build them from the reference logic in sections 2-3, not from old view counts.
- Script: claim/question → familiar comparison → irony thesis → picture sentences → turn every 10-15 s → mirrored ending aimed at the viewer.
- Visuals: one colour world with an arc, a new picture per phrase, big layered sets with depth (not icons), hero words only on key nouns, silence screens at the turns.
- Voice: the most natural voice available (Chatterbox clone was the approved one; Kokoro is the fallback).

## 5. Owner's lessons, 27 Nov 2026 (these override the order of work above)
1. **Packaging before script.** First fix the idea, the title and the cover line, based on what people instinctively react to. The cover text IS the hook and the first spoken line. Only then write the script.
2. **Reward in 3-6 s.** Hand over the shocking fact early ("free money made people work MORE" + the number by ~4 s), THEN open a gap with a question or fear. Teasing alone is too slow (the shock at ~8 s was late).
3. **One topic, deep.** Study ~10 videos on ONE topic (how big creators open, turn and close it), build our master script from that, and tell the body as a short story.
4. **Mix 3 reel types:**
   - Quips: myths and shocking stats, for shares and follows (all we've made so far).
   - Tips: practical how-tos, for saves.
   - Clips: personal or behind-the-scenes, for comments.
5. **Word discipline:** ~75 words = 30 s, ~150 words = 60 s. Cut every word that isn't needed; if it's still long, split it into two reels. Short, punchy lines (natural Hinglish for Hindi scripts).
